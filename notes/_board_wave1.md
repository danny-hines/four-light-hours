## sun (critical) final 6.5

Round 3 is a clear step up. Nothing failed to load, the page logged no errors, and the GPU time is inside budget (full disc 2.67-2.86 ms, skim 2.6-2.7 ms with only=sun,post at 1600x900). The full disc at exposure=0.0003 is a convincing HMI-style white-light Sun. Its limb darkening now slightly exceeds the HMI reference (G channel at r=0.97: 61 vs HMI 80, centre 178 vs 172), the limb turns redder as it should, faculae brighten toward the limb, and nothing clips. The wide corona at 0.002 reads as an eclipse photograph: a pearly K-corona, helmet-streamer bulbs, fine rays reaching the frame edge, and prominences on the limb. Far away the Sun is an intense HDR point that bloom turns into a star (Jupiter at 40 R, Neptune at 20 R, 1 AU). Skim motion is smooth and steady with no popping: frame-to-frame correlation is 0.967-0.971 at 1 s steps and 0.980-0.983 at 0.5 s steps close in. In the film (t=12-45) the beats work and the departure has no artifacts.

What keeps it from SVS grade is a set of CG tells in exactly the hero close-ups. (1) The prominences are saturated hot-pink tubes combed into smooth, coherent strands with white cores. They read as neon or light-painting trails, not cloudy, fragmented H-alpha plasma, and the grazing view is the worst case. (2) The sunspots are geometric: near-perfect circular or elliptical penumbrae with a hard, smooth outer edge and coarse radial wedges at mid range. Close up they become a symmetric 'iris' with a drawn worm for a light bridge, and the groups sit in straight, evenly spaced rows. At full disc every spot is a bullseye. (3) Granulation hue swings too far (lemon-yellow cells on orange lanes). Close in, the cells are smooth, flat-topped blobs ('giraffe' or cheese texture). At mid range it is a uniform speckle with no meso/supergranular modulation. (4) In the skim, the spicule fringe is a uniform comb and the lifted inner corona is a flat grey fog. (5) The corona rays are a uniform radial-blur fibre texture with straight wedge streamers. Inside the corona, looking away from the Sun, a compact singular swirl artifact appears at a fixed world direction. It comes from the corona shell (it disappears with sunmask=8) and is not hit by the current cut. Floor clearly met; target partly met: impressive at full-disc and wide distances, with obvious tells in the close views.

1. **[structural]** Prominences read as neon tubes or light-painting: saturated hot-pink/magenta strands with white-hot cores. The strands are uniformly smooth, run coherently along the whole arch at a constant tube width, and have sharp edges. There is no diffuse, cloudy mass, no fragmentation into knots or blobs, and no fuzzy envelope. Seen grazing in the skim, they become braided ribbons like glow-stick trails. They are in every hero frame from t=18 to 40 s of the film.
   - fix suggested: (a) Shift colour toward eclipse red: HA about (1, 0.10, 0.10). Soft-clip the optically thin term with EM/(EM+k) at a far lower ceiling than 24, or apply sqrt(EM), so cores reach pale pink, never white. (b) Break strand coherence: multiply each strand's density along its arc length by a 2-3 octave, high-threshold noise (filling factor 0.15-0.35) so it fragments into knots and gaps. Give each strand a lognormal brightness (sigma about 0.8) and a radius that varies along its length (pinched at the feet, fatter at the apex). (c) Add a diffuse envelope around each rope: a Gaussian 3-5x the strand radius at about 10-20% of the strand emissivity, modulated by low-frequency noise, so the arch is a cloud with threads inside rather than wires on black. (d) Soften the tube edge: integrate a Gaussian radial profile instead of a hard erf cylinder. (e) Hedgerows: replace the smeared vertical streaks with knotty, segmented threads of varying brightness.
   - evidence: shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_limb_arch.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/x_promzoom.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/x_graze.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_full_rprom.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_skim_lcorner.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/film_t36.png
2. **[structural]** Sunspots look geometric. Penumbrae are near-perfect circles or ellipses of uniform width with a hard, smooth outer edge and no interdigitation with the granulation. At mid range the penumbra is a flat orange disc with coarse radial wedges. Up close it is a perfectly symmetric radial 'iris' with a drawn worm-like light bridge. Groups are laid out in straight rows with even spacing, and at full disc every spot is the same bullseye of round umbra plus round halo.
   - fix suggested: (a) Outer penumbral boundary: r_out(az) = r0*(1 + 0.15*noise(3-8 lobes) + 0.05*fbm(fine)). End each filament at its own radius (per-cell random, ±15%) so granules intrude between filament tips. (b) Add azimuthal curvature and twist to the filaments that varies with radius and per spot. Put bright heads on the inner third and darken toward the outer edge (inner penumbra about 0.85, outer about 0.70 of the quiet Sun). Bundle the filaments into clusters with gaps. (c) Make umbrae irregular (domain-warped, often 2-3 cores in leaders). Build light bridges from the granulation itself with a variable-width mask, not a separate drawn feature. (d) Lay out groups with a Joy's-law axis plus random lateral offsets (±20-40% of the spacing). Make followers fragmented clusters of pores and small spots with rudimentary, one-sided penumbrae. Use a power-law size distribution. (e) At full-disc LOD, keep the penumbra footprint irregular and asymmetric so it isn't a donut.
   - evidence: shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_limb_spot.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_skim_spot.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/x_spot.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_full_spots.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/limb.png
3. **[functional]** The granulation's hue modulation is too strong (lemon-yellow cells on orange lanes, about 10-15% RMS luma contrast plus a strong hue swing). Close in, the cells are smooth, flat-topped blobs with little interior structure, which reads as giraffe skin or cheese. At mid range (limb view, skim) the texture is a uniform speckle with no mesogranular or supergranular brightness modulation, so it looks like a procedural noise tile rather than a solar surface.
   - fix suggested: Apply CHROMA only to the large-scale limb and spot temperature terms. For granulation-scale fluctuations, use CHROMA about 1.0 or below, so the luminance contrast stays but the hue stays nearly constant (real ±100-200 K hardly shifts colour). Add a 5-10 Mm mesogranular and 30 Mm supergranular luminance modulation of 2-4%, a lognormal cell-size distribution, and close-range interior structure: bright edge rims, occasional dark dots, and small fragmentation lanes with more amplitude. Keep the narrow lanes, but break them with chains of bright points and pockets.
   - evidence: shots/critic/sun/round3/x_gran_close.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_skim_gran.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_limb_gran.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/skim.png
4. **[functional]** Skim limb and sky: the spicule forest is a uniform comb or fur fringe of same-length pink spikes along the whole horizon. The 'lifted' inner corona above the horizon is a flat neutral-grey fog, so the sky reads as an overcast backdrop rather than luminous plasma.
   - fix suggested: Spicules: draw heights from a broader heavy-tailed distribution (2-15 Mm). Cluster about 70% of the jets into bushes with empty stretches between them, vary brightness per jet (lognormal), and taper the tips into a diffuse glow. Sky: make offLift a strong function of height above the limb (bright pearl within about 0.05 R, falling as about h^-1.5), tint it warm pearl (1, 0.95, 0.88), and modulate it with the streamer and ray structure so the sky isn't a flat grey sheet.
   - evidence: shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_skim_lcorner.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_skim_rcorner.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/film_t36.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/x_graze.png
5. **[cosmetic]** The corona's fine structure is a uniform radial-blur fibre texture covering every position angle at similar amplitude. Streamers are straight wedges with no curved helmet geometry or narrowing cusp, and polar plumes don't curve like dipole field lines, so the wide shot reads somewhat as a zoom-blur filter rather than the Druckmüller-style magnetic structure in real eclipse corona photographs.
   - fix suggested: Trace the striation coordinate along field lines of a dipole plus source-surface model (closed loops below about 2-2.5 R, open lines bending toward the equator in streamers and toward the poles in holes) instead of pure radial position angle. Give the rays a heavy-tailed amplitude distribution (a few bright discrete rays, mostly weak background). Concentrate the fine rays in streamer stalks and polar plumes, and smooth the quiet regions.
   - evidence: shots/critic/sun/round3/wide.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_wide_inner.png, shots/critic/sun/round3/crop_wide_rightedge.png

## mercury (major) final 6.5

Round 3 Mercury clearly meets the floor and gets close to the target in the shots the film actually uses. No Mercury module errors in any run. The half phase, the pole/terminator view and the film's terminator pass (t=62-66) look like real near-terminator imagery. Crater relief is crisp, the small-crater field is dense and clustered, peaks catch light beyond the terminator, and shadows are long. The colour is a natural brownish grey. The full disc is flat with no limb darkening (correct opposition photometry), and the isolated crescent is clean. Consecutive film frames (65, 65.033, 65.067) show no popping or shimmer.

It is not yet top-studio quality, and the remaining tells are in the hero low pass:
(1) Procedural craters do not obey the DEM terrain shadow. A crater interior shows as a lit disc with a hard circular outline inside a black terrain shadow (film t66). Craters sitting entirely inside terrain shadow glow at 6-14/255 while the shadow around them is 0. Complex craters read as flat pale decals with stamped T-shaped or bird-shaped central-peak shadows.
(2) In close views with a high or mid Sun the surface is mush: the 7.5 km/px base map's cloudy blotches, pale ring outlines and almost no relief shading (contract: no blur when filling the frame).
(3) The mid-ground and near-horizon terrain in the low pass goes soft and smeared, with parallel row lines near the skyline, and the skyline is an almost smooth arc.
(4) Crater walls are airbrushed smooth, and crater shadow outlines are perfectly smooth ellipses at 10 km altitude.
(5) The full disc is low in contrast with a salt-and-pepper speckle, and its rays and young craters are weaker than in the reference.

The huge white haze band behind Mercury in the required view=body shots comes from the asteroids module (its belt ring is not zeroed outside the director). I isolated it with only=mercury plus single modules. It is absent from the film and is not Mercury's fault, but it also causes the glowing ring on the night side in crescent.png.

1. **[functional]** Procedural crater interiors ignore the DEM terrain shadow and use inconsistent fill light. A crater floor shows as a lit disc with a hard circular edge inside a black terrain-shadow region, in the film's hero low pass. Craters lying wholly inside terrain shadow glow grey (6-14/255) above the surrounding 0-black shadow, so they read as pasted lenses.
   - fix suggested: Multiply each procedural crater's whole lighting (direct and fill) by the far- and near-field DEM shadow evaluated at the crater's rim-plane height, i.e. the local reference surface before the bowl is carved. If the rim plane is in shadow, so is the floor. Keep the crater's own analytic self-shadow as min() on top. Scale the fill term by the sunlit fraction of the crater's own walls (their DEM visibility), so a crater inside terrain shadow gets zero fill rather than a few percent.
   - evidence: shots/critic/mercury/round3/film_t66.png (crater at ~990,595), zoom_t66_crater.png; x_term_long.png + zoom_term_long.png (sampled: crater interiors 6-14 vs surrounding shadow 0)
2. **[functional]** Complex and degraded procedural craters read as flat pale decals: a hard thin circular outline, a smooth uniform interior, and a stamped T-, bar- or bird-shaped dark 'central peak shadow' instead of an elongated pointed shadow.
   - fix suggested: Give the central peak a real height profile (a cone or Gaussian with ridged noise) and shadow it by marching 6-8 analytic samples of that profile toward the Sun, so shadows taper to points along the light direction. Fade the degraded-crater rim into the terrain with a wide smoothstep (0.15 r), not a thin step. Add wall terraces and slumps (rings modulated by angular noise) so the interior has shading structure.
   - evidence: shots/critic/mercury/round3/verylow.png (craters at ~1300,520 and ~870,740), zoom_verylow.png; zoom_t66_crater.png (T-shaped bar)
3. **[functional]** Close views with a high or mid Sun are blurry texture mush (target: 'no blur at 0.1 R altitude'). The 7.5 km/px base albedo's low-frequency blotches are magnified ~70x and read as clouds or concrete. Craters appear only as pale ring outlines, with almost no topographic shading at 40-65 degree Sun elevation.
   - fix suggested: When the base texel footprint exceeds ~6-8 px, fade the base map's mid and high frequencies toward their local mean, using a lower mip as the 'mean' and lerping the map toward it by footprint. Replace them with synthesized albedo tied to the crater field: bright immature rims and ejecta, slightly dark floors, plus a power-law-spectrum albedo noise matched to MDIS NAC statistics. Check that the photometric partition does not flatten topographic shading at g=25-50 degrees. Hapke macroscopic roughness (theta-bar ~16-20 degrees) would restore crater-wall contrast at mid phase.
   - evidence: shots/critic/mercury/round3/x_highsun.png, zoom_highsun.png (dist=1.1 phase=25), x_limb.png (dist=1.15 phase=50)
4. **[functional]** Detail falls off and smears in the mid-ground and near the horizon during the low pass. Terrain beyond ~3 altitudes goes soft, with stretched horizontal streaks. Parallel row lines run near the skyline, and the skyline is an almost featureless smooth arc at 0.085 R altitude.
   - fix suggested: Replace the isotropic shading footprint (0.55x long axis) with anisotropic filtering: take 4-8 taps along the major axis of the footprint ellipse (EWA-style) and choose the crater/roughness LOD from the minor axis. Crater octaves then survive at grazing angles without aliasing. For the skyline and row structure, march the horizon against a min-max DEM pyramid (conservative skipping, then a fine refinement) and refine the hit with a secant step, so step quantization does not print rows.
   - evidence: shots/critic/mercury/round3/film_t64.png + zoom_t64_far.png; x_horizon.png + zoom_horizon_left.png (stair-step skyline and row bands); zoom_motion_triplet_far.png
5. **[cosmetic]** Shadow shapes look CG at close range. Crater shadows on the floor are perfectly smooth ellipses, crater walls are airbrushed smooth (no talus, gullies or boulders), and large DEM shadows read as soft rounded ink blots rather than long shadows with serrated edges.
   - fix suggested: Add rim-height noise (angular harmonics 3-12 plus fbm along the rim) to the occluder profile used in the crater shadow test, so shadow edges on floors and walls follow the serrated rim. Make walls rougher than the flats (higher roughness amplitude and radial gully noise where the slope exceeds ~20 degrees). In the far-field march, add the procedural rim heights of the largest crater octave to the occluder.
   - evidence: shots/critic/mercury/round3/x_ultralow.png + zoom_ultralow_crater.png (dist=1.004); lowpass.png (blobs at ~430-600,350-560)

## venus (major) final 6.5

Round 3 Venus clearly meets its floor and it holds up at disc scale. No module failures appeared in any run. At phase 20 the day disc sits close to the Mariner 10 reference: creamy (centre about sRGB 220/214/204, not clipped), with structure that is subtle but present (luminance std 2.2%, p1-p99 about 10%). The tan absorber patches, the darker equatorial band and the polar spiral streaks only show under stretch, which is what the brief asks for. The physics is mostly right. The terminator is broad and smooth at phase 90. Cusps extend around the night side at 150. Phase 178 gives a complete ring. The 150-degree cusp close-up has a nice layered twilight limb. The night side is truly black (sRGB 0.2). At 400 radii the crescent still reads as a 1-px sliver. Super-rotation is correct: cross-correlation between days=0 and 0.03 gives a coherent -34 px drift (r = 0.86-0.94), which matches the predicted 33.6 px for a 4-day retrograde period. What keeps it from top-tier is mostly the hero beat and close range. (1) The crescent and ring of light are dim, neutral grey and don't glow. At 172 degrees the ring covers only about 270 degrees and is missing on the anti-sun side. (2) The convective cells added this round, seen at 0.15 R, read as a crazed crack network on otherwise soft, blurry clouds. At low altitude the deck is a featureless fog plane (0.8% std). (3) The haze, limb and crescent turn grey or slightly cyan rather than warm cream. (4) The planetary-scale Y and the polar collar are not legible without stretching. Separately, the body views carry a bright grey ecliptic band from the asteroids piece, and in the film's crescent beat post glare and ghosts make the night disc muddy. Neither is Venus-owned, but both hurt the shot. Performance is at the 240 fps vsync cap with Venus filling the screen.

## earth (critical) final 6.5

Round 3 Earth & Moon, judged from 30+ renders (all 7 required views plus 17 extras and film frames t112-160). No module failures and no page errors in any log. The floor is clearly met: real continents, oceans, advected clouds, atmosphere, city lights and a Moon. The best frames are strong. The film's Blue Marble at t124 and the dist=3 disc read close to Apollo 17 at a glance, with good land detail (Okavango and Etosha visible, seasonal snow in Scandinavia, polar night in the Arctic). The night pass at t136 is the best shot of the piece: a layered orange/blue/green twilight limb over warm city clusters, close to the ISS reference. The orbital sunrise with aim=sun (el 74.5 and 73.8) has a convincing layered limb and a flattened, reddened Sun. Clouds advect the right way: measured against land between days=0 and days=1, tropical clouds move about 20 px west and southern mid-latitude clouds about 31 px east, with no shear. Earth turns correctly through days=0, 0.25 and 0.5. City lights stay on the night side. The Earthrise at t156/t160 is lit and crisp. Below NASA SVS grade, from most to least harmful: (1) from 1000 km down, clouds are visibly procedural: sponge-like decks with grey holes, soft out-of-focus blobs at 255 km, and comb-like rows of dashes in trade cumulus. (2) The hero disc is washed out next to Apollo 17. Deep-ocean 5th percentile is 71 vs 32, cloud 99th percentile is 210 vs 244, and the ocean is steel-blue (40,87,132) instead of navy (26-43,58-69,102). (3) Terminator reddening reads as muddy brown, not pink/orange, with no blue twilight band from mid range. (4) The ocean glint is a grey haze patch dimmer than the clouds (core 165 vs 196). (5) The lunar ground at Earthrise looks stamped with same-looking crater decals. (6) The Moon at default exposure is muddy (phase-60 lit mean 44 vs reference 136; terminator view peaks at 16/255). (7) Three required views show nothing of their feature (black sunrise, no glint in frame, Earthrise against black lunar night), although the features do exist at other geometries. Performance is borderline but within noise: the full-screen disc measured 2.8-8.4 ms median on a shared GPU, minimum 2.0 ms. Cross-piece, not scored here: the sky's zodiacal light lays a grey fog over most Earth and Moon frames (15_earthrise_p85.png, 08_sunrise_aimsun745.png, 01_approach.png).

1. **[structural]** Clouds look procedural at close range (low orbit to ~1000 km). Closed-cell stratocumulus reads as a uniform sponge/foam with mid-grey holes. Trade cumulus is a uniform stipple of similar flecks. At 255 km, cumulus are soft blurry blobs with no crisp edges, no lit vs. shaded side, and no visible ground shadow. At mid range, trade cumulus shows comb-like rows of short horizontal dashes. Several fleck elements look like motion-blurred brush strokes. These are the clearest giveaways in every low-orbit beat.
   - fix suggested: (a) Stop driving cell size and blob strength everywhere from one Worley scale: modulate cell size (10-40 km), anisotropy and coverage with a low-frequency regime field plus the map's local coverage gradient, so neighbourhoods differ (clusters, streets, open/closed transitions). (b) Closed cells: bright, per-cell-hashed cloud polygons separated by thin clear-ocean walls (threshold F2-F1 to narrow walls), not grey interior holes. Open cells: cloud rings around ocean-coloured centres. (c) Below ~1 km footprint, let the logistic edge width fall to ~1 px; the s_unres floor keeps edges blurry at 255 km. Give each resolved cumulus a cloud-top height and do a 4-6 step height-field march toward the sun for a lit side, a self-shadowed side, and a cast shadow offset along the sun azimuth by h*tan(zenith). (d) Remove the dash periodicity: align any streak stretch to the local wind from the flow field, cap anisotropy at ~2:1 below 20 km scales, and randomize stretch phase per cell.
   - evidence: shots/critic/earth/round3/04_glint.png, c04_cells.png, 12_leo_horizon_day.png, c12_clouds.png, 23_land_close.png, 10_glint_inframe.png, c10_glint.png, 05_days0.5.png, c05h_flakes.png, c24_term.png
2. **[functional]** Hero Blue Marble disc is flat and washed out next to Apollo 17. Deep-ocean blacks are lifted about 2x, cloud whites are dimmer, and the ocean reads milky steel-blue instead of deep navy, so the whole disc looks seen through haze. This is the film's signature Earth shot (t116-124).

## audio (major) final 6

Round 3, audio. The engineering is excellent and the music is good but not exceptional. I rendered the score myself (render.mjs into shots/critic/audio/round3) and checked it with my own BS.1770 / true-peak / spectral script. Results: true peak -2.34 dBTP, integrated -18.0 LUFS, LRA about 10 LU, DC about 4e-6, 0 NaN or subnormal samples. My detector found no clicks, and the only silences are the fade from black (0-2 s) and the tail after 446 s. The 10 warp legs show up as broadband risers. Each chapter has its own key (the chroma top pitch classes follow the harmonic plan) and a centroid from 280 Hz to 1.9 kHz. On the live page shot.mjs --t 10 loaded with no errors and no module failure. live.mjs gave 0% underruns at all 6 dense moments, and pause, auto-suspend, resume, mute, a 25-seek scrub and the composed ending all behaved correctly. The code is good: seeded and deterministic, per-category plus global voice caps (offline peak 130 voices), sweeps that disconnect finished voices, generations that crossfade, and ramped pause and mute.

The weaknesses are in the music:
1. **Melody is mostly fragments.** About 31 of the 45 melodic events are 2-3 notes long. The longest statement is 6 notes, and nearly every phrase lasts 3-7 s inside a single chord, so there is never a long, singable line.
2. **Register and harmonic rhythm barely change.** The voice-led line that the pads and choir read from tops out around E4 in 8 of 12 worlds and never goes above A4. Chords change every 6-9 s in almost every world. As a result the Sun, Earth, Mars, Saturn and finale have nearly the same long-term spectrum (self-similarity at or above 0.9).
3. **The lead instruments sound synthetic.** The piano is one cached buffer per pitch, with the same timbre at every velocity. The solo voice is a fixed formant filter over a wavetable.
4. **The warp legs repeat one gesture.** All 9 are the same hiss column over a held chord. The loudest moment of the film is the Jupiter-to-Saturn riser (-10.9 LUFS short-term), not a musical climax. The builder's own render now exits 3 on the current 441.5 s cut (leg dip -1.2 dB, crest +5.3 dB, two melody notes under +6 dB of salience).

Smaller issues: each seek dips 5-11 dB for about 150 ms. Earth, Mars, Saturn and the finale have a presence hole around 2 kHz. The bass line sits in octave 1. live.mjs compares the ending against a stale WAV. Overall this reads as an impressive, well-mastered generative ambient synth score. It does not yet sound like a composed planetarium score.

1. **[structural]** The melodic writing is mostly fragments and never builds a long line. Of the 45 melodic events, about 31 are 2-3-note fragments: SIXTH about 14 times, L_HEAD about 10, B about 8. The longest statement is 6 notes (A_EXT at the eclipse), and almost every phrase lasts 3-7 s inside one chord. The emotional anchors (Earth arrival, Earthrise, eclipse, pale blue dot) each get a 4-5-note cell, then the texture returns to pads with bell and glass dabs. With nothing to follow over 7.4 minutes, the score reads as ambient wallpaper rather than an emotional film score, and this hurts the film more than anything else in the audio.
   - fix suggested: Let a line span several chords. Add a `span: [chordFrom, chordTo]` mode to realizeLines/emitLines and write 3 real tunes of 8-16 bars with an antecedent and a consequent: (a) Earth arrival, piano A theme spun out over Dmaj9-Gmaj9-Bm9-Gm6/9 and answered by a cello-range countermelody; (b) the Saturn eclipse, with the choir soprano carrying A_EXT into a second phrase that climbs to the high point of the film; (c) the finale, a full restatement with the countermelody and a fuller orchestration growing across the last 30 s.
